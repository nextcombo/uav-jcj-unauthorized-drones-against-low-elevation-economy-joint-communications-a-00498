# [UAV+JCJ] Unauthorized Drones Against Low-Elevation Economy - Joint Communications and Interference Based on MIMO Cellular System [with MATLAB Code]

To ensure the vigorous development of low-altitude economy and to combat unauthorized drones (UAVs) is an indispensable task. The widespread deployment of base stations has tremendous potential for joint communication and interference (JCJ). In light of this, this paper investigates beamforming joint design based on dual-function multi-input multi-output (MIMO) cellular systems to support both legitimate users' communications and countermeasures against unauthorized UAVs simultaneously. We first formulate a JCJ problem, relax it through semidefinite relaxation (SDR), to obtain a solvable semidefinite programming (SDP) problem, which SDR provides the necessary steps to simplify complex JCJ designs. Although the solution to the relaxed SDP problem does not directly address the original problem, it provides valuable insights for further refinement. Based on these insights, we design a new constraint specifically for the structure of the SDP problem to ensure that the solution adheres to the rank-1 constraints of the original problem. Finally, we validate the effectiveness of the proposed JCJ scheme through extensive simulations. The results confirm that the proposed JCJ scheme can effectively operate when the total number of legitimate users and unauthorized UAVs exceeds the number of antennas.
Introduction
The concept of Low Altitude Economy (LAE) is considered an economic system that integrates various low-altitude aviation activities involving unmanned and manned aircraft, including Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) and Electric Vertical Take-Off and Landing (eVTOL) aircraft. It is expected to promote a variety of low-altitude services in industries such as transportation, environmental monitoring, agriculture, and entertainment, with the potential to bring significant economic and social benefits. This potential has sparked a surge in research interest [2] [3] [4] [5] [6].
To ensure the success of LAE, it is not only necessary to ensure continuous wireless communication with authorized drones but also to implement necessary countermeasures against unauthorized drones. There are typically two types of countermeasure technologies: physical capture and jamming [9]. Physical capture methods are efficient and cost-effective but can be unfriendly to pilots. Jamming is the most commonly used method for neutralizing drones entering restricted areas, which is divided into four categories [7]. The first category involves emitting non-structured signals similar to noise to reduce the Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR) of unauthorized drones. In [10], partial noisy jamming was implemented to cut off the control link between the drone and its controller. The second type requires broadcasting deceptive Global Positioning System (GPS) signals to guide unauthorized drones away from the restricted area. The third category involves hacking into the communication protocols of unauthorized drones and generating false commands to control them to leave the restricted area. The fourth category involves passive jamming, which involves modifying the environment's signals using passive devices to cause destructive superposition on the receiver side. A common method is designing phase shifters for reconfigurable smart surfaces (RIS) [15], [16], [17], [18].
A. Prior works on ISAC have mostly focused on utilizing a unified hardware platform and/or waveforms to simultaneously achieve de-modulation of communication data and extraction of noisy observational target parameters [20], [21], [22]. In ultra-large MIMO systems, the user's activities, channels, and positions are jointly perceived in [23]. A compression sampling perspective is proposed to facilitate ISAC processing. The uplink channel and position of the user equipment (UE) in terahertz ultra-large array system are obtained together in [25]. An index-based modulation-centric ISAC system centered on sensing is proposed. Given the potential of drones to empower a wide range of industries, there has been much work integrating drones with ISAC [1], [27], [28].
However, the above ISAC lacks adequate countermeasures for unauthorized UEs. [29] has extensively discussed various methods of interference and anti-interference. To address the challenges of interference during communication, dedicated waveforms have been developed to maximize detection capabilities [30]. Additionally, researchers have studied the use of interference signal echoes for sensing while maintaining communication functions. This method allows for dynamic resource allocation based on perception results [31]. In [32], deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methodologies are employed using dedicated interference devices and frequency hopping spread spectrum to interfere with the devices. Research has also been conducted on full-duplex systems with a single UE and UAV, where an unauthorized UAV is detected and interfered with by uplink signals from the UE [33]. Moreover, the simultaneous communication and interference under CSI errors have been rigorously studied [34]. [35] proposes a new method using optical devices to generate electromagnetic signals, achieving secure communication while performing radar distance and speed deception interference. Real-world tests have shown the effectiveness of low-energy narrowband signals in disrupting fifth-generation networks [36].
In [37], the concept of integrating radar, communication, and jamming was proposed for the purpose of disrupting unauthorized UEs. The premise of this study is centered on security, assuming that the entity subject to interference is a listener. This assumption is restrictive and may not necessarily apply to scenarios involving all unauthorized UAV flights where unauthorized UAVs may not engage in eavesdropping. Therefore, further research is needed to overcome the challenges of common jamming techniques for unauthorized drones. Recently, a generalized framework for integrated communication and jamming (ICAJ) has been proposed, which enhances communication and jamming within a system to achieve more reliable communication and efficient jamming [38], [39]. However, the aforementioned studies assume separate communication data streams and jamming streams. In reality, both communication data streams and jamming streams can be designed jointly to achieve more coordinated ICAJ.

In addition to these active interference techniques, passive methods have also attracted attention. As collaborators, RIS can effectively cover blind spots and improve signal quality. However, when RIS operates as an interference source, it can remodel the characteristics of multipath signals through it, thereby generating destructive interference at the receiver [15], [16], [17]. While these advancements are being made, research on anti-interference has also made significant progress. Liu et al. [40] studied the dynamic interactions between an interferer and a base station (BS), where the BS continuously adjusts its beamformer to respond to the interference capabilities of the interferer. Sun et al. [41] focused on designing robust passive beamforming using RIS to counteract interference, even if angle information is inaccurate. Additionally, a different approach was proposed, which involves using additional transceivers in the environment to remodulate the interference signal with embedded structured information for potential use [42]. The continuous progress of interference and anti-interference technologies complement each other, driving continuous advancements in both fields.
From a fundamental perspective, integrating ISAC with drone countermeasures relies on downlink beamforming as one of the key technologies. There are many classic schemes. For full-digital beamforming, there are well-known schemes based on channel inversion (CI) [43], linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE) [44], and optimization-based schemes [45], [46], [47]. For hybrid beamforming, there are schemes based on semidefinite programming (SDP) [48], orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) [49], and alternating minimization [50]. Thanks to the development of deep learning, some advanced data-driven beamforming schemes have emerged [51], [52], [53]. However, as far as we know, there is currently no universal downlink beamforming solution for scenarios where communication and unauthorized drone countermeasures are integrated.
B. Our Contributions Our contributions are summarized as follows. 
We developed a joint communication and interference (JCJ) model and formulated an associated optimization problem that demonstrates how the BS can meet basic communication requirements while effectively countering unauthorized drones with minimal power expenditure. Through theoretical analysis of the formulated problem, we concluded that additional interference flows are not necessary. The effective countermeasures against unauthorized drones can be seamlessly integrated into the data stream of UEs. 
After further in-depth analysis of the structure of the proposed JCJ optimization problem, we developed an additional constraint. Specifically, the real part of the sum of any diagonal elements along parallel to the main diagonal of the complex symmetric matrix (including cyclic wrapped diagonals) should not be less than the fraction of the matrix trajectory. This constraint ensures that the solutions obtained through SDP satisfy rank-1 constraints. Simulations confirmed the effectiveness of this constraint.
The proposed JCJ scheme does not require additional signal flows to interfere with unauthorized drones, making it effective when the total number of UE1 and unauthorized drones exceeds the number of transmit antennas. In contrast, traditional schemes are completely ineffective under these conditions. Furthermore, when the number of transmit antennas is less than 32, the proposed JCJ scheme consistently outperforms the traditional CI scheme.

Conclusion:
To address unauthorized drones ubiquitously using widely deployed MIMO BS, this paper focuses on beamforming joint design to establish a dual-function system that interferes with unauthorized drones while communicating with legitimate UEs. We first formulate the JCJ problem and then convert it into a standard SDP problem using SDR. By analyzing the structure of the formulated problem, we find that no dedicated interference flow is required for countering unauthorized drones. We then propose an innovative constraint to ensure that the solution to the rephrased problem satisfies the rank-1 constraint. Finally, extensive simulations validate the correctness and effectiveness of the proposed JCJ scheme, yielding interesting insights. The results confirm that the proposed JCJ scheme is beneficial when there are fewer antennas or when the total number of UEs and unauthorized drones exceeds the number of antennas.
However, the JCJ scheme proposed in this paper does not include a sensor function. The joint design of communication, sensing, and countermeasures provides a promising avenue for future research. Additionally, in more practical scenarios where communication and interference frequency bands differ, it is another direction worth exploring to study how to coordinate communication, sensing, and interference. Moreover, for the array structure of BS using hybrid beamforming, the proposed method can serve as an input to many existing solutions for hybrid beamforming, which may increase complexity and potentially lead to suboptimal performance. Therefore, based on the structure of the problem, designing a hybrid beamformer directly to use communication signals to interfere with unauthorized drones offers a valuable direction for future research. Furthermore, utilizing the beam-focusing characteristics of large arrays could lead to more effective interference tasks.
